Exact finite-operator facts
--------------------------
For frozen twin heads Q1,Q2 on the SAME action proposals, define
  Q_min = min(Q1,Q2), Q_mean = (Q1+Q2)/2,
  h(a) = Q_mean(a)-Q_min(a) = |Q1(a)-Q2(a)|/2 >=0.
Monotonicity and translation equivariance of the empirical expectile imply
  min_i h(a_i) <= e_tau(Q_mean(a_1:N))-e_tau(Q_min(a_1:N)) <= max_i h(a_i).
Thus the mean-root target is at least the min-root target before projection.
Projection is monotone and nonexpansive, so its target displacement lies
between0 and gamma*mask*the unprojected root displacement. This is a statement
about frozen learned predictions, not a label for true critic error.

For a FIXED behavior generator, let F be the expected finite-N scalar Bellman
operator. For either reducer A=min or mean, the ideal synchronous twin operator
is T_A(Q1,Q2)=(F(A(Q1,Q2)),F(A(Q1,Q2))). Both are gamma-contracting in sup norm.
After one exact synchronous application both output heads are equal. At a
fixed point A(Q,Q)=Q, so BOTH reducers have the SAME unique scalar finite-N
fixed point. This fixed point need not equal population-expectile IQL's value.

Therefore the observed min-versus-mean performance difference is not explained
by a superior ideal fixed point. It concerns persistent head disagreement,
approximation/optimization/sampling errors, target clipping and their learning
path interactions. Extra V fits do not remove those errors; V is unused in
these two finite backups anyway. The ideal proof does not establish neural
SGD/EMA convergence or oracle action-ranking correctness.

A persistent added Bellman perturbation d can accumulate geometrically with
scale d/(1-gamma). This provides a reason to investigate small systematic
head-reduction displacements when gamma is high. Actual logged head gaps are
state-dependent and not oracle bias labels. Do not substitute a fixed-data
RMS or one frozen root gap into a sup-norm bound and claim a measured B_k.
Clipping and changing states can also cancel or truncate contributions.
