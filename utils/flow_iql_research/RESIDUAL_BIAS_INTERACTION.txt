Residual control and actual policy improvement

The supplied Analysis the Stability of IQL.pdf defines the score residual by
G_tau(q,V_tilde)+zeta=0. Its local decomposition (14) is
  g_k = D e_tau(Q_k)[epsilon_k] + zeta_k/kappa_k + r_k.
Its propagation equations (16)-(20) retain the critic-fit response B_k and give
upper norm bounds. The residual channel can have a larger condition number.
These results do not give a monotonic policy-improvement guarantee when one
signed error channel is reduced while the other channels and B_k can change.

An exact local counterexample

Consider a start state with actions leading to G or B at zero immediate cost.
At either destination the behavior action a is uniform on [-1,1], followed by
termination. Rewards are .1*a-.15 at G and .1*a-.1505 at B. Thus G is better
by .0005 under any common continuation action or common action distribution.
Both action-value distributions are atomless. Rewards lie within [-1,0].

The population expectile of uniform[-1,1] is
  c_tau=(sqrt(tau)-sqrt(1-tau))/(sqrt(tau)+sqrt(1-tau)).
Consequently V*_G=-.15+.1*c_tau and V*_B=V*_G-.0005.

Give G two frozen critic heads Q*-b and Q*+b, with b=.001. At B both heads
equal Q*. Mean aggregation equals truth in this constructed example; minimum
aggregation shifts the exact expectile at G downward by b. Suppose the fitted
V_G initially equals V*_G. Its fitting displacement relative to the perturbed
min-Q expectile is +b, exactly canceling the critic-induced displacement -b.

Before residual correction the start targets prefer G. After perfectly fitting
V to the min-Q population expectile, the start targets prefer B, because
  V*_G-b < V*_G-.0005.
The resulting actual return loss under any common continuation action is
gamma*.0005=.0004995 for gamma=.999. Clipping to the valid conservative return
range [-1000,0] is inactive. Using mean twin-Q in the exact expectile restores
the correct ordering in this particular constructed case.

This respects the paper's residual sign convention. The score at approximate
V_G is .1/4*[tau*(1-t)^2-(1-tau)*(1+t)^2], with t=c_tau+b/.1; zeta is minus
this score. Kappa at the exact root is sqrt(tau*(1-tau)). At tau=.9, zeta is
.000298 and zeta/kappa=.000993333, close to the exact displacement .001.
The remaining difference is the local expansion's higher-order term.
The numerical record also evaluates tau=.99.

This is a one-update counterexample with frozen perturbations, not a claim
about asymptotic learning. It shows why reducing a sensitive residual channel
alone need not reduce the actual signed disturbance or improve the policy.
It neither refutes the paper's propositions nor establishes that real critics
are unbiased when averaged.

What the actual OGBench evidence says

Holding the actual V2 BC flow fixed, changing only the deployed critic source
to the finite-expectile-backup critic changes the last-three CPU50 success:
  Antmaze-large: .060000 -> .486667
  Cube-double:   .313333 -> .000000
  Puzzle-3x3:    .926667 -> 1.000000
These are three pilot tasks, not a nine-task result or extra training seeds.
The controller change also changes the behavior-distribution approximation;
it is not an oracle intervention on the true conditional residual alone.

In 128 common heldout next states at 100K, 300K, 800K and 1M, both observed
next-action Q and generated-action Q fall together for Cube-double. The
finite-backup critic approaches -2000 and loses most within-state generated-
action contrast. Replacing one generated action by an observed action has a
small frozen expectile shift in these checkpoints. This weakens the current
anchor hypothesis; it does not prove that the behavior generator is accurate.
A flat or otherwise wrong learned critic can hide proposal-quality differences.

For identical frozen proposals, averaging the two target heads instead of
taking their minimum raises the finite expectile. This follows from
  min(Q1,Q2)=(Q1+Q2)/2-|Q1-Q2|/2
and monotonicity of the expectile. The computed difference is not a true-Q
bias measurement. Its product with gamma/(1-gamma)=999 is an illustrative
fixed-operator sensitivity scale, not a prediction of actual neural values.

The next registered experiment changes only this backup aggregation. It keeps
the same tau=.9, gamma=.999, nine proposals, BC training, unused auxiliary V
fits, return projection and min-Q deployment. Public FQL and the user's FQL
port default to mean target-Q aggregation. No per-task coefficient is added.
Overestimation can increase, so complete late evaluations and independent
training seeds are still required. The fixed-proposal contraction argument
does not bound the neural fitting response B_k or prove training convergence.
