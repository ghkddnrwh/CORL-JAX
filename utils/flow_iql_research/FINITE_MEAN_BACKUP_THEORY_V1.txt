Finite mean-head expectile backup: statements and their scope

Implemented target
Given a frozen behavioral flow at an update, drawN=9actions a_j at s'. Define
x_j=(Q_target,1(s',a_j)+Q_target,2(s',a_j))/2. The finite expectilee_tau(x)
is the unique root of
  tau*sum_j max(x_j-v,0) - (1-tau)*sum_j max(v-x_j,0) = 0.
The code solves this piecewise linear scalar equation by sorting and checking
allN+1intervals, with centering for numerical conditioning. The implemented
target is projection_[L,0](r+gamma*mask*e_tau(x)), L=-C/(1-gamma).
The empirical root is exact up to floating-point arithmetic; the logged score
is a numerical check on those sameNproposals, not an independent oracle test.

Removal of one fitting path
A learnedV need not minimize even this finite conditional objective exactly.
Using the root directly removes the learnedV fitting approximation from this
particular target. The sampled flow distribution need not equal the unknown
behavior distribution. Nine sampled actions are not an exact population
expectile, and Q_target remains estimated. This intervention does not remove
critic, coverage, density, transition-sampling or function-approximation errors.

What twin mean changes exactly
For two real numbersq1,q2,
  (q1+q2)/2-min(q1,q2)=abs(q1-q2)/2.
Thus, with the same target heads and same candidate actions, every mean-head
proposal is at least the minimum-head proposal. Monotonicity of the expectile
implies root_mean>=root_min; translation equivariance also gives
  0<=root_mean-root_min<=max_j abs(Q1_j-Q2_j)/2.
This is a same-update deterministic comparison, not the eventual difference
between separately trained neural trajectories. Calling this difference a
known true-Q bias requires assumptions on the heads' calibration. For example,
conditionally unbiased heads imply the min is pessimistic in expectation,
whereas the mean preserves that unbiasedness before the nonlinear expectile.
Neither unbiasedness assumption was established by these offline logs.

An ideal operator statement
For0<tau<1, e_tau is monotone in every component and translation equivariant.
If max_j|x_j-y_j|<=d, then y-d<=x<=y+d componentwise, so
  e_tau(y)-d<=e_tau(x)<=e_tau(y)+d.
Therefore the scalar expectile is1-Lipschitz in the sup norm. Averaging two
heads and interval projection are also1-Lipschitz. For fixed proposals at each
next state, or a fixed proposal distribution with the appropriate population
expectile, the resulting exact Bellman operatorT consequently satisfies
  ||TQ-TQ'||_infinity<=gamma*||Q-Q'||_infinity,
using0<=mask<=1 and coupled proposals in the finite comparison. Its fixed point
belongs to that expectile/proposal operator, not necessarily optimaltrueQ.

How errors can accumulate under additional assumptions
If an approximate iteration can be written as Q_(k+1)=TQ_k+epsilon_k for one
fixed operatorT, the ideal sup-norm recursion is
  ||Q_k-Q_*||<=gamma^k||Q_0-Q_*||
               +sum_(i=0)^(k-1) gamma^(k-1-i)||epsilon_i||.
Uniformepsilon yields epsilon/(1-gamma). A constant signed scalar discrepancy
can similarly accumulate geometrically. These equations explain why persistent
local errors matter atlargegamma. They are not observed estimates of B_k.

Limits of transferring this statement to the actual learner
The real flow changes during training, proposals are resampled, target heads
use EMA, regression uses offline minibatches and neural optimization, and the
training data do not cover allstate-actions. Projection bounds targets rather
than allnetwork outputs. The ideal contraction does not prove neural training
stability, eliminate population residuals, or ensure good deployed action
rankings. Even finite-min with numerical root scores~1e-6 failed on several
tasks. The deployment also uses best9byminimumonlineQ rather than the backup
expectile policy, so improved backup control and final success remain distinct.
