Exact next-success boundary in the finite expectile backup
========================================================

This candidate makes ONE change to the finite-min-Q expectile backup. It does
not combine independent critics, temporal averaging, boundary auxiliary loss,
GMM, rank-flow or half correction. All tasks retain tau=.9, gamma=.999, nine
behavior-flow proposals, min twin-Q, projected targets, and best9 deployment.
The existing two auxiliary V fits remain for a matched diagnostic/control
implementation; neither learned V nor its optimizer enters this Q backup.

Ordinary finite backup:
  y = r + gamma * current_mask * empirical_expectile_tau(target_minQ(s',a_1:9)).
Boundary version:
  y_B = r + gamma * current_mask * (1 - certified_next_success) * root.
Both use the same final projection onto the known return interval. There is no
additional coefficient, loss, data resampling or RNG stream.

Why zero is known here
---------------------
Installed OGBench single-task relabeling defines success from the CURRENT state
(qpos/button state), with r=0 and mask=0 there for every action. Thus Q(s_goal,a)
and every policy/expectile continuation at that state are exactly0 in this
implemented masked task. This is not a generic interpretation of timeout/done.
The original trajectory-cut terminals and these reward masks are distinct.

Regular OGBench data retain next_observations and current-state rows. A label is
certified only when the original trajectory-cut terminal[t] is0 AND the next
observation exactly equals observations[t+1]. Then masks[t+1]==0 certifies a
next-success state. Noncut observation mismatch aborts; never approximately
match observations or shift across a trajectory cut. The final next state of a
trajectory is not a retained current-state row and remains unknown. This uses
only training data already supplied to the learner, with no reward thresholds
or environment-dependent tunable values. No validation transitions are trained.

Relation to propagated error
----------------------------
On a certified active edge (current_mask=1, next_success=1), the exact Bellman
target is r. The original target's boundary error is gamma*root, and the new
target removes exactly that error on this edge. This does not identify errors
at other states, repair action ranks, or guarantee a policy return gain.

For a fixed behavior generator, empirical expectiles are1-Lipschitz in their
input values under the sup norm. Multiplication by mask*(1-c) in[0,1] and
interval projection are nonexpansive. Hence the corresponding ideal fixed-
generator boundary operator is at most gamma-contracting. On functions with
the correct zero boundary it agrees with the original operator on covered
edges. Learned neural optimization, a changing behavior flow, finite proposals
and uncovered next-state labels retain their usual limitations.

Coverage is deliberately narrow
------------------------------
The train active-edge counts from the complete adjacency audit are:
Ant321/1M, HMM209/2M, HML128/2M, Soccer54/1M, CubeS448/1M,
CubeD17/1M, Pz3 68/1M, Pz4 ZERO, Scene49/1M. Most consecutive success-state
rows already have current_mask0, so overriding their next value changes no
target. Sparse entry edges may explain a null result; this method does not
increase their training weight. Empty coverage is exactly the original method.

Initial pilot environments are HMM, Ant and CubeD, all seed0. These test the
previous collapse, the largest navigation entry coverage, and the much rarer
CubeD entry coverage respectively. No nine-task mean exists until all9 complete
tasks from one common recipe are available. Last3 CPU50 (800K/900K/1M, episode
seeds42..91) remains the sole primary metric; intermediate diagnostics do not
replace it. A discovery gain still needs independent training-seed confirmation.
