Boundary error and the return-floor geometry
==========================================

This note interprets the registered diagnostics. It is not a neural convergence
claim, an oracle for interior values, or a benchmark performance result.

Why a bad constant critic can fit most sparse-cost transitions
------------------------------------------------------------
Let the largest per-step component cost be C and L=-C/(1-gamma). Consider the
constructed constant critic Q(s,a)=L and its constant finite-proposal expectile
V(s)=L. On a nonterminal transition,

    r + gamma L - L = r + C.

Thus every nonterminal transition with r=-C has exactly zero TD target gap.
For a mask-zero success with r=0 the target is0, and the gap is -L. Partial
progress rewards greater than -C also break the constant-floor consistency.
Projection to[L,0] does not remove these facts. It constrains target range,
while success requires the correct value at a particular boundary state.

The constant floor is NOT a fixed point of the complete episodic Bellman
operator: success boundaries explicitly contradict it. This construction
explains the weak training signal in the bulk of sparse-cost data; it does
not prove that stochastic neural learning will converge to that critic.

With a constant proposal-Q value, the finite expectile equals that constant
for every tau and sample count. More proposals or larger tau cannot restore
missing contrast at such a frozen critic. They may change earlier learning
dynamics, so the ongoing tau.99 experiment remains necessary.

Measured training-data geometry
------------------------------
audit_reward_floor_geometry_v1.py evaluates the above identity on all nine
training and validation datasets, using each task's known return bound.
All navigation/Soccer and Cube-single nonterminal rewards are the minimum
cost, hence their nonterminal progress-floor signal fraction is0.
The corresponding fractions are about .0514 for Cube-double, .9974 for
Puzzle3,1 for Puzzle4, and .9166 for Scene. Cube-single has about5.33 success
rows per uniform256 batch; HMM/HML have only .50/.31 and Cube-double .15.

These differences provide a reason to inspect boundary supervision and reward
density together. They do not uniquely identify the cause of any result.
Different dynamics, representation demands, behavior coverage and critic
approximation remain possible explanations. Puzzle4 has no complete-success
rows; the new boundary auxiliary correctly disables itself there.

A certified empirical error contribution
----------------------------------------
On the verified OGBench state-based mask-zero boundary, true masked-task
Q(s,a)=0 for every action. If f is the boundary frequency in an empirical
dataset and E_goal is the RMS of both learned Q heads on all its goal rows,

    empirical RMS Q error >= sqrt(f) * E_goal,
    frozen empirical sum-head critic MSE >= 2 f E_goal^2.

The second inequality follows because the regression target on those rows is
exactly0 regardless of learned V, target Q, generator or discount. Every
other row contributes a nonnegative squared error. The first uses only the
known true boundary value and says nothing about true interior Q.

For the actual500K finite-backup HMM/HML models, the training-data RMS error
lower bounds are6.71/7.64 and the sum-head regression-MSE lower bounds are
90.04/116.84. On their validation empirical measures the RMS lower bounds are
42.45/29.15. These are NOT bounds under an unknown deployment occupancy and
must not be compared directly to a moving minibatch-window loss.

Relation to the residual and propagation hypothesis
---------------------------------------------------
The finite backup computes its proposal expectile algebraically; its auxiliary
learned V cannot influence the Q/BC update. Consequently boundary failures in
this branch cannot be explained by that learned-V fitting residual entering
the backup. Critic approximation, generator/finite-proposal effects and their
Bellman propagation remain. A finite-proposal score near0 is compatible with
wrong values at known success states.

One isolated Q-boundary refit tests local repair after collapse while keeping
BC,V and target Q fixed. Its boundary-only loss intentionally omits TD, so it
cannot establish what happens when repaired labels are propagated through
coupled learning. The separately registered from-start boundary-augmented
finite-backup pilots test that coupled question. A failure of the frozen
refit to improve return is not a rejection of the preventive intervention.

The ideal fixed-generator exact scalar backup remains a gamma contraction.
That statement does not establish contraction of neural projection, optimizer
updates or a changing learned generator. Removing a V fitting displacement
does not eliminate these other approximation and propagation channels.
