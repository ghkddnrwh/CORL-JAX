At every Q update
1. Draw9actions at each next state from the unweighted behavioral flow.
2. Average the2old target-Q heads for each action. Solve the tau-expectile of
   these9scalar values exactly, up to floating-point arithmetic.
3. Form y=r+gamma*mask*root and project to[-C/(1-gamma),0]. Fit both online
   Q heads once to this target; fit BC once with ordinary flow matching.
4. Keep the official old-online-Q EMA. Deployment draws9BC candidates with
   10Euler flow steps and chooses by minimum online twin-Q, as in IFQL.
The frozen implementation also performs2learned-VAdam fits for historical
clock/diagnostic controls. That V enters neither the bootstrap nor deployment.
Do not claim these extra fits are necessary or remove them silently from this
validated runner. The method has one learner, not independently trained ensembles.

Scientific scope
The finite root removes the learned-V fitting approximation from the sampled
backup. It does not remove behavior-density, finite-sample, critic or model
errors. Mean heads remove the explicit repeated minimum; no true-Q bias oracle
was measured. Target projection does not guarantee stable neural optimization.
Known-bound scalar ideal operators and the actual evolving neural learner are
different objects. Small fitting/finite-root scores alone did not ensure returns.
The original2V-fit Gaussian recipe did not improve the original-gamma nine-task
average (.188889 before and after). The selected finite-flow method is a later
algorithm, not a claim that this first modification suffices for every setting.
High tau=.99 remains unsolved; final finite-mean results reported here use.9.
No equal-compute, paper-bitwise-reproduction, statistical multi-seed superiority
over matched FQL, universal stability, or SOTA claim is made.
