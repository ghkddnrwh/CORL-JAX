Real-data rank-weighted policy flow: exploratory comparison
=========================================================

Question and scope
------------------
The synthetic-label rank-flow pilot distills actions selected from a learned BC
generator. A bad generator or its poorly valued outliers can become policy
labels. This comparison keeps the same IQL V2 Q/V updates, unweighted BC flow,
policy architecture, optimizer clocks, and deployment procedure, but changes the
policy objective to weighted flow matching on actual dataset actions. It is a
new hypothesis, not a demonstrated improvement or a claim of novelty.

The whole value intervention is two same-minibatch V Adam fits against a frozen
old target-min-Q, fresh-V bootstrap, and projection of the Q target to the known
return interval. Gaussian AWR in the official-style baseline uses OLD ONLINE
min-Q minus OLD V. The target critic supplies V targets, not the AWR advantage.
The policy methods here do not use V in their actor losses.

One global policy rule
----------------------
For each data pair (s,a), generate nine actions from the old unweighted BC flow.
Evaluate all actions with the old ONLINE twin-min-Q. Attach independent Uniform
tie breakers to the data action and the comparator actions; order by (Q,tie).
Let C be the number of comparators below the data action. Set

    w(C) = 9 if C=9, 1 if C=8, and 0 otherwise.

Train a separate policy flow with mean[w * squared FM velocity error], using the
real data action as its endpoint and independent Gaussian/time samples. Add the
ordinary unweighted BC-flow FM loss with coefficient one. These losses optimize
separate parameter blocks. Stop policy gradients through weights/comparators/Q.
Do not divide the policy loss by the realized minibatch weight sum. Deploy one
sample from the policy flow with ten Euler steps, without a critic or BC flow.

This is a comparison of policy objectives, not another combination of separate
correction heuristics. Nine comparators and order nine are the same on all pilot
tasks. There is no exponential temperature or actor dependence on V residuals.
Common training setting: expectile .9, gamma .999, public network/Adam settings.

Why these weights have a statistical interpretation
---------------------------------------------------
Fix s, a critic, and a conditional behavior distribution mu. First assume the
BC flow samples exactly mu. The augmented (Q,Uniform) score has an atomless CDF
F even when Q has ties. Conditional on the data action's augmented score x,
the nine comparator outcomes are independent Bernoulli(F(x)). The weight is the
average of nine estimators, each equal to 9 times the indicator that the data
action beats a subset of eight comparators:

    w = (9/9) sum_{j=1}^9 I[data beats every comparator except j].
    E[w | s,a,tie] = 9 F(x)^8.

The best of nine independent mu actions has exactly density 9 F(x)^8 relative
to the augmented mu law. Integrating out the random tie breaker gives the same
action law as random-tie best-of-nine selection. Thus weighted real-action FM
and FM on independently selected best-of-nine labels have the same population
objective under these assumptions. Their finite-data estimators differ.

The data action has a uniform rank among ten iid augmented actions, so

    E[w]=1, E[w^2]=(1+81)/10=8.2,
    asymptotic ESS/batch_size = 1/8.2 = 5/41, about .122.

The bounded weights are stable to positive affine changes in Q, but sparse.
About 80 percent of examples have zero weight in the ideal iid calculation.
This variance is a concrete disadvantage compared with dense selected-label FM;
it is not hidden by batch normalization of the weights.

Relation to expectile policy extraction
---------------------------------------
The bounded-action order-statistic argument in RANK_FLOW_METHOD.txt shows that
E[max_{i<=N} X_i] >= expectile_tau(X) when N >= tau/(1-tau). Order nine satisfies
this at tau=.9. With exact Q_tau, an exact behavior generator, and an exact
policy distribution fit, this gives the same ideal policy-improvement argument
as the selected-label policy. It does not prove performance relative to FQL or
the selected-label teacher. Order nine has no analogous guarantee at tau=.99.

What can invalidate the interpretation
--------------------------------------
The actual BC flow approximates mu. Then the conditional comparator CDF is F_BC
and the weighted data target is proportional to
    mu(a|s) * E_tie[9 F_BC(Q(s,a),tie)^8].
Its statewise mass need not equal one. Finite-capacity learning can also change
the effective weighting of states. This intentionally differs from best-nine
synthetic labels and is precisely the failure channel under investigation.
Learned Q may rank actions incorrectly, including on the data. Random ties only
remove deterministic tie bias; they do not recover missing action information.
Flow approximation error, sparse weights, target drift, finite data and Euler
discretization remain. Small residual loss or small actor FM loss is not proof
of a useful policy. An exact student of a fixed ideal teacher does not exceed
that teacher by virtue of this derivation.

Diagnostics and fair comparison
-------------------------------
Training logs include weight mean, ESS, fractions of 0/1/9 weights, zero-mass
minibatches, observed/comparator Q contrasts at the same state, exact Q ties,
candidate Q/action contrast, and unweighted policy data-FM loss. No zero-mass
minibatch is silently resampled. Existing rank-flow distribution diagnostics
compare policy/BC/best9-BC distributions and report independent teacher sampling
resolution. Here the synthetic best9 flow is a REFERENCE policy, not the exact
training-label distribution when the BC generator is misspecified. The named
selected-target FM probe is likewise a reference objective. Auxiliary teacher
CPU50 evaluations do not replace primary policy CPU50 evaluations.

Existing inference/frozen-V helpers instantiate the original RankFlowAgent.
This is valid only because both models have identical serialized arrays,
configuration, sample_actions, selected_teacher_targets, and Q/V computations.
They have different actor losses. Tests must verify exact state restoration and
identical deployment from both templates, and the actual runner is separately
tested for full/resumed training. Source hashes include this new core, its
runner, rank_flow_core_v1, policy_core, core, and all vendored dependencies.

Each pilot runs 1M critic updates. Primary score is CPU50 mean at 800K/900K/1M
with independently reset seeds42..91. Pilot environments are Antmaze-large,
Cube-double and Puzzle3. No per-task winning-method mixture or incomplete
nine-task mean is eligible. All benchmark conclusions require actual results;
CPU implementation tests and estimator simulations are not training replicates.
