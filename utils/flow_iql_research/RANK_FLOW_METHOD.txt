Selected-action policy flow: recipe, rationale, and limits

Status: CPU validation complete; three GPU pilots queued. No GPU result yet.

Keep the official-style IQL V2 value learner and behavior flow. At each state
draw nine independent actions from the old behavior flow and choose the one
with highest old online minimum twin-Q. Treat this selected action as a target
sample for a separate policy flow. Train that flow with ordinary conditional
flow matching and independent Gaussian/time draws. The BC teacher still fits
dataset actions; neither the selected target nor Q receives the policy-flow
loss gradient. Both flow losses have coefficient one on disjoint parameter
blocks. There is no new temperature or per-task policy coefficient.

At deployment draw one action from the policy flow with ten Euler steps. This
amortizes the best-of-nine selection distribution; it does not learn a pointwise
MSE map from an independent latent to a random selected action. Flow matching
is used because the selected-action distribution can remain multimodal.

Q, V, target-Q and BC initialize exactly as the official IFQL control. The new
policy-flow parameters start as a copy of BC-flow parameters. The BC FM noise
and time keys retain the official IFQL three-way split. Extra targets/noises
use folded-in keys. Replay, Q/V update counts and target EMA are unchanged
mathematically; long GPU bitwise identity is not assumed.

The common recipe is tau=.9, gamma=.999, nine teacher proposals, one deployed
policy-flow sample, ten Euler steps, and the same published widths/normalization.
V fits twice on the same frozen old target-Q batch, uses fresh next-V, and
projects the Bellman target onto the known return interval. A single Q, BC,
and policy-flow Adam update follows. The new actor loss does not use V, so
an additive state-only V residual cannot directly change its ranking targets.
Propagated V errors can still change Q rankings.

Why nine proposals is a coherent common choice

Here is a supporting derivation, not a novelty claim. Let X be a bounded scalar
with CDF F, e=e_tau(X), and r=tau/(1-tau). The expectile equation gives
  A_minus = E[(e-X)_+] = r E[(X-e)_+] = r A_plus.
Let M_N be the maximum of N independent copies of X, and p=F(e). Then
  E[(e-M_N)_+] = integral_{-infinity}^e F(x)^N dx
                    <= p^(N-1) A_minus,
  E[(M_N-e)_+] = integral_e^infinity [1-F(x)^N] dx
                    >= (1+p+...+p^(N-1)) A_plus.
Since the geometric sum is at least N*p^(N-1), N>=r implies
  E[M_N]-e >= [1+p+...+p^(N-1)-r*p^(N-1)] A_plus >= 0.
The constant-X case follows directly. Atoms/ties do not invalidate this CDF
integral argument. For tau=.9, N=9 is sufficient in the ideal population result.

If Q_tau is the exact fixed point of the behavior-expectile Bellman operator
and the behavior generator is correct, best-of-nine yields a policy pi_9 with
E_{pi_9} Q_tau >= e_tau(Q_tau). Thus T_{pi_9}Q_tau >= Q_tau, and monotonicity
and discount contraction imply Q^{pi_9} >= Q_tau. A policy flow matching pi_9
would inherit that ideal comparison. This is conditional reasoning: learned Q,
an approximate behavior generator, imperfect flow fitting, changing targets,
and finite Euler steps remove the guarantee in the actual neural experiment.

An exact student matching a fixed teacher has the same policy distribution and
return as that teacher. The theorem does not promise improvement over IFQL's
own best9 selector or over FQL. Any measured gain over the current teacher
would require explanation through target-history smoothing, generalization,
or another actual learning/approximation difference. A reduction in inference
work alone does not satisfy the research performance objective.

The ranking target is invariant to a state-only offset and a positive rescaling
of its Q scores in exact arithmetic. If scores have an atomless distribution,
its action density is N*mu(a|s)*F_s(Q(s,a))^(N-1). The sampling-and-selection
definition remains valid with ties. This is rank reweighting, not an exponential
advantage weight whose scale or threshold depends directly on fitted V.

Diagnostics needed for interpretation

Training records BC FM loss, policy FM loss, selected teacher Q gain relative
to the pool mean, winner margins, near-tie fraction, candidate Q/action spread,
and selected-action saturation. Frozen diagnostics must compare distributions,
not pointwise MSE between unrelated policy and teacher latents. Useful checks
include model-Q expectation/regret, action-distribution energy distance and
heldout selected-target FM loss. These are model-based diagnostics, not true
Q-error measurements or guarantees of environment return.

If policy-flow samples match the teacher distribution but environment success
is poor, inspect critic/proposal quality. If teacher best-of-nine succeeds while
the policy flow fails, inspect policy fitting, target drift, mode coverage and
Euler discretization. Frozen behavior/critic interventions can separate these
causes. The full 800K/900K/1M CPU50 score remains the performance criterion.

Compute and scope

Teacher generation costs nine ten-step BC flows during each training update,
plus ranking and two flow losses. Deployment uses one ten-step policy flow;
plain IFQL best9 uses nine ten-step flows and nine Q evaluations, while FQL
deploys a one-step student. These are logical counts, not wall-clock timings.
The method is not claimed to be compute matched to FQL or novel.

The prototype files are rank_flow_core_v1.py and train_rank_flow_v2.py. The
v2 runner explicitly pins inherited policy_core.py; the earlier v1 runner is
retained only as a short CPU validation artifact. Existing training sources
were not modified. New model factories require frozen_diagnostics_v4.py;
watch_frozen_v3.py dispatches older kinds to their existing v3 helper.
