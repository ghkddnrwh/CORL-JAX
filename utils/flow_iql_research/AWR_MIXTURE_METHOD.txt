AWR mixture policy with the same IQL value correction
====================================================

This is an alternative policy family, not another loss added to the flow
policies. It is exploratory and has no benchmark result yet.

Keep the official-style IQL Q/V objectives and V2 correction: two V Adam fits
against frozen old target-min-Q, fresh-V bootstrap, known-return Q-target
projection, one Q/actor update and the public old-online-Q target EMA.

Keep the Gaussian IQL actor's public AWR objective:

    A(s,a) = min Q_online_old(s,a) - V_old(s)
    w(s,a) = min(exp(alpha*A), 100)
    L_actor = -mean_data[ w(s,a) log pi_theta(a|s) ].

Evaluate the exponential as exp(min(alpha*A, log100)) to avoid unnecessary
overflow. Do not normalize by the minibatch weight sum. No additional entropy,
flow matching, Q-action-gradient, distillation, or behavior teacher is trained.

Change pi from one fixed-variance Gaussian to a five-component conditional
diagonal Gaussian mixture. The architecture is the existing mixture BC model:
public GELU hidden layers, actor without LayerNorm, and log std clipped to the
public Actor range[-5,2]. Alpha values are inherited from the public Gaussian
baseline, not tuned for these pilots. Five components are shared across tasks
and reuse the earlier unweighted mixture control's count.

At evaluation temperature0, choose the component with the largest mixture
probability and use its mean, clipped to[-1,1]. Do not query Q at deployment.
This is a deterministic latent-component rule. It is not claimed to find the
global mode of the full mixture density. No per-task decoder choice is allowed.

Why test this
--------------
For a fixed-variance single Gaussian and fixed state, the population weighted
log-likelihood optimum has mean E_mu[w*a]/E_mu[w]. When good data actions have
multiple separated modes, this average can fall between them. A mixture can
represent multiple modes and deploy one component instead of averaging them.
This is a representation hypothesis, not a guarantee of higher return.

Unlike the previous mixture BC experiment, this actor is trained directly on
advantage-weighted data and does not sample32 behavior actions for Q selection.
The comparator of interest is Gaussian IQL with the same value controller,
not just the old unweighted mixture proposal model. Because the mixture also
learns component variances, a gain cannot be attributed purely to component
count without a learned-variance K1 control. That control is implemented as
kind awr_gaussian, but is not claimed to have a benchmark result.

V errors are still relevant: without the cap, a fixed state's exp(-alpha*V)
factor cancels from the ideal normalized conditional action law, but changes
state weighting in finite shared-function fitting. The cap can also change
relative action weights. This method therefore complements the value study;
it does not claim to remove all actor sensitivity to V error.

What is controlled and logged
-----------------------------
Copy the initial Q/V/target-Q parameters and agent RNG from the public Gaussian
initialization. Actor loss gradients into those parameters are zero. Four CPU
updates reproduce the public Gaussian V2 Q/V parameters exactly in the unit
test. This does not certify identical long GPU trajectories: the existing
graph-dependent BC arithmetic finding applies to such claims.

Training records AWR mass, ESS, clipping/underflow proxies, zero-mass batches,
mixture entropy, effective components, weighted component responsibilities,
component spread and std-bound saturation. Frozen-Q diagnostics also compare
independent train and validation likelihoods, posterior/component usage,
greedy-component versus conditional-mean MSE, and learned-Q contrasts. Better
likelihood or lower MSE does not certify better actual return.

An unregularized mixture can collapse its component use or push variances to
the allowed lower bound. These are possible failure modes to diagnose, not
reasons to silently tune a different regularizer for each task.

Pilot protocol
---------------
Antmaze-large, Cube-double and Puzzle3 use one common K5 recipe, tau .9 and
gamma .999, for1M Q updates. Primary evaluation is CPU50 at800K/900K/1M with
independently reset seeds42..91. Early curves are progress only. A selected
recipe must be tested uniformly across all nine environments and confirmed
on fresh training seeds before a FQL/ReBRAC superiority claim.

The public fixed-std Gaussian remains the baseline. No completed or active
training source is edited; new runs use awr_mixture_core_v1 and
train_awr_mixture_v1. Frozen diagnostics dispatch this family through v5,
while existing policy families retain their existing diagnostic helpers.
